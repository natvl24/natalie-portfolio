Why I started it

Everyone I knew had moved to New York and still felt like a stranger in it. I wanted to join a community that didn't have one specific hobby or niche. Meeting people online was easy; showing up somewhere was the hard part. I wanted a brand that removed the friction of the first hello and that plan already exists, all you have to do is come.

What I did

Built the brand voice and visual identity, then ran the whole loop: a content calendar that drives signups, event programming from venue and vendors to day-of logistics, and outreach to partners. Every flyer, deck and story frame is mine. Attendance sheets and Instagram metrics decide what we program next.

What I'd do differently

I priced early events on instinct instead of on cost, and learned the hard way that a sold-out room can still lose money. Now every event starts as a model. I'd also have captured emails from night one instead of leaning on the algorithm to reach the same people twice.

[01]  Visuals

A look you can spot mid-scroll

I built a consistent visual system around a defined palette, two typefaces, film-grain photography, and a corkboard-inspired collage style, then carried it across every flyer, invite, and recap post.

The monthly events board became a signature format: one recognizable image that holds the full calendar and gives the community something easy to save and share. Reusable templates also cut production time to about twenty minutes per event while keeping every asset unmistakably For The Plot.

[02]  Taglines

Copy that lowers the stakes

The name itself says what we are about, come for the story and make real life connections. Every event gets a line written to make showing up alone feel normal. It should feel like an invitation or a small dare, never a pitch. I write for the person screenshotting it to send to a friend, so the tagline has to make sense with no context. Lines that get replies become recurring formats, which are the ones that don't get cut.

[03]  Partnerships

Trading audience for room

I pitch venues on the weeknights they want to fill and brands on a room full of the exact people they want to reach. They get an engaged audience, content they can repost, and their product in people's hands. I run the partnership end to end, from outreach and the pitch deck to terms, day of activation, and a recap with attendance and reach. That way, the next conversation starts with proof. Most partners come back for a second event.

Why I started it

It started as a place to document living with my family and cats during quarantine in 2020. It turned into a real audience faster than I expected. Staying on it taught me the thing no course does, which is how attention is earned in the first two seconds, over and over, and nothing you did last month carries you.

What I do

I come up with the idea, write it, film it and cut it in CapCut, where the pacing and the sound do most of the work. Then I repost it to Reels so it reaches more people. I also write my own media kits and negotiate my own contracts with lifestyle and hospitality brands.

What I'd do differently

I chased trends before I had a format of my own. Looking back, the videos that actually worked were the ones only I could have made. I'd pick two formats I could repeat and let the trends fit into those instead of the other way around. I'd also move my audience somewhere I actually own a lot sooner.
