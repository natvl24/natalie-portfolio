Strategy

Objective

Turn matches into meet-ups.

Making the initial connection is only one part of friend-finding. The campaign focuses on what happens next by making it easier for matches to turn a conversation into a real plan, and ideally, a second one.

Insight

The hard part isn't the hello, it's the where.

I've seen this through my own events: people are much more likely to show up when the plan, place, and activity already exist. Removing some of that logistical friction makes taking the first step feel easier.

Big idea

Give every match a third place.

Not home and not work, but a familiar neighborhood spot where Bumble BFF already has a table, an offer, or a recurring event. Instead of simply encouraging people to make plans, the campaign gives them one to join.

Channel roles

TikTok recruits · Instagram organizes · OOH reminds · IRL delivers

Each channel has a clear role. TikTok makes the challenges of adult friendship relatable and shareable, Instagram houses the calendar and event content, neighborhood and transit OOH keeps the campaign visible in everyday life, and IRL experiences turn the idea into something tangible.

Partners

Five partner types, each built around a recurring event format and chosen because they already make it easy for people to gather around a shared activity. The partnership gives venues a reason to activate lower-traffic time slots while Bumble BFF provides programming, promotion, and social content they can reuse.

Coffee shops

Weekday mornings. Low-pressure, easy to join, and simple to leave after an hour if the connection isn't there.

Fitness studios

A class gives people something to do together before they have to make conversation, which takes some pressure off the first meeting.

Bookstores

A monthly club with the book chosen in-app creates a built-in conversation topic and a natural reason to come back.

Bars

Early-evening meetups with a reserved table and a host make arrival feel more structured and less awkward.

Creative spaces

Ceramics, screen-printing, flower arranging, or similar workshops give people something to focus on while conversation happens naturally.

The loop

01See

Creator content or an OOH placement introduces the idea on the way home.

02Plan

An in-app prompt suggests a nearby Third Place happening that week.

03Show up

A reserved table, named host, and conversation prompts make the first meeting feel easier.

04Post

Attendees share the experience, and Bumble BFF resurfaces the strongest community content.

05Return

The same event returns the following week, giving people a natural reason to meet again.

Design decisions

Type

Bold rounded sans, handwriting for the human bits.

Headlines use one bold rounded sans in sentence case, large enough to read quickly in-feed or from a distance. Neighborhood, day, and time sit underneath in smaller, widely spaced type. Handwriting appears sparingly on calendar notes and prompts like “Are you in?” so the information feels organized without losing the warmth of a personal invitation.

Color

Warm enough to invite, bright enough to notice.

A warm yellow palette inspired by Bumble BFF's visual world runs through the campaign. Cream creates a softer base for calendars, Stories, and event materials, while saturated yellow is reserved for moments that need more attention, like transit posters and table signage. Coral is used sparingly to underline, point, and highlight key actions.

Imagery

Small groups, mid-conversation.

Four or five people sit around one table, looking at each other instead of the camera. The groups stay intentionally small and the settings familiar, whether it's a café, studio, bookstore, or stoop, so the experience feels like something you could actually join rather than a crowd you'd have to break into.

Layout

The idea first, then the where and when.

Awareness pieces lead with one simple line so the campaign idea lands before the logistics. Event assets add a clear details band with the neighborhood, date, time, audience, and RSVP. That separation lets the campaign work both as something memorable and something useful enough to screenshot.

Copy

Name the offer, never the deficit.

“Give every match a third place” focuses on what Bumble BFF can offer rather than what someone might be missing. From there, every line has a purpose. “Making friends as an adult is awkward. Just show up.” acknowledges the friction. “What are you doing Thursday?” turns it into a plan. “You already have something in common. You're both here.” carries the idea into the event itself. “Join the plan” closes with a simple next step.

Format

One job per channel, one format per job.

4:5 carries campaign ideas and calendars in-feed, while 9:16 is built for Stories that introduce the event, prompt interaction, and close with an RSVP link. OOH and in-venue materials are designed around their specific placements rather than resized from social assets. Event cards and table signage can also be adapted digitally so the same recurring night stays recognizable across physical and social touchpoints.
